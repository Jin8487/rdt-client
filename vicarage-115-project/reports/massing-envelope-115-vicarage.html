2 · Why this envelope holds

The pre-app response (Leah Russell MRTPI, 7 Jul 2026) gave away more than it refused. In writing, the council has conceded: (1) the property is detached — which unlocks the 8 m prior-approval right, the 4 m standard rear depth and the 50 m³ loft allowance; (2) "there is no objection to the principle of a two storey rear extension"; (3) "the 45 Degree Code would be met from both neighbouring properties", taken "from the end of [No.117's] conservatory rather than the original window openings". That disposes of the amenity case. What remains is a single, judgement-based design objection — "not subservient… reduce by 2m to 4m" — citing City Note LW-16 of the Birmingham Design Guide SPD 2022.

Two verified facts defang that objection. First, no numeric depth cap for two-storey rear extensions exists anywhere in the Design Guide SPD 2022, the Development Management DPD 2021 or the BDP 2017 — "4 m" is one officer's unquantified judgement (adversarially verified; the only geometric test in the policy set is the 45° Code, which she concedes is passed). Second, subservience is an appearance test, not a floor-area ratio: inspectors allow extensions of "notable depth" where the ridge is set down, the roof matches and flanks are set in — see APP/P4605/D/25/3373138 (834 Walsall Road, Perry Barr): a two-storey side and rear extension allowed on appeal against Birmingham under the very same Design-Guide wording.

The strategy is therefore not to argue with her — it is to route everything except the first floor around her. Prior approval (8 m ground floor), Class B (loft) and Class E (outbuildings) are statutory rights where design opinion is legally irrelevant. Only the first-floor depth passes through discretionary planning, where you start from her own written concession of 4 m and push from a position of strength.

3 · Element by element

3.1 Ground floor rear — 6.00 m secured; 8.00 m as tested upside

Detached-status correction (owner-confirmed): 115's garage abuts No.117's structure for its whole length. If that link makes the house not a "detached dwellinghouse" for GPDO purposes (appeal practice on link-by-garage houses is divided; the officer's letter calling it "detached" is helpful evidence but not determinative), the A.1(g) limit is 6 m, not 8 m. Strategy: notify 6.00 m (valid for any dwellinghouse — unchallengeable), and separately test the 8 m question by s.192 LDC if the extra 2 m matters; take 6.7 m through the planning application meanwhile. Class A.1(g): a single-storey rear enlargement may extend beyond the original rear wall by 6 m (any house) / 8 m (detached), max height 4 m, eaves ≤3 m where within 2 m of a boundary (A.1(i)) — both flanks here are. The existing rear block need not be demolished: under A.1(ja) it can be retained and subsumed provided the joined total stays within every limit — which makes the existing block's eaves height (measured from photos at ~2.9–3.1 m) the single most important survey dimension on the project. If it exceeds 3.00 m within 2 m of a boundary, rebuild that roof edge or demolish-and-rebuild within the envelope.

Procedure: notify the LPA (£249 fee), neighbours get 21 days, the LPA gets 42. An objection from No.113 does not block anything — it only obliges the LPA to assess "the impact … on the amenity of any adjoining premises". Design, subservience and the Design Guide are legally out of bounds (the prior-approval matters are confined to those in the Order — CAB Housing v SSLUHC [2023] EWCA Civ 194). Her pre-app letter conceding the 45° Code is your amenity evidence. If the LPA is silent for 42 days, you may lawfully proceed (deemed consent). Since SI 2019/907 there is no completion deadline. Bank it afterwards with an s.192 certificate (or s.191 once built).

Independent geometry check (BRE-method, computed and shown in the working papers): a 3 m-high wall at ~3.1–3.3 m lateral from each neighbour's nearest habitable window subtends ~24–26° — below every BRE screening line — and rough Vertical Sky Component ratios are 0.96–1.00 (BRE target ≥0.8 or ≥27% VSC). There is no credible amenity case against a ≤3 m-eaves single-storey run at these separations.

2005 file now fully recovered — three findings. (1) Calibrated metrology on the approved drawing 381/02 A puts the approved extension at 4.39 m ±0.08 deep × 10.53 m wide, rear fascia 2.42–2.72 m: the block standing today is the 2005 permission as built — lawful, no certificate needed, and its eaves sit inside the 3.00 m PD limit, so the joined 8 m total complies on the drawn figures. (2) The as-existing survey 381/01 shows that by March 2005 the house already had a full-width ~2.9 m flat-roof rear addition and the two-storey side wing over the garage — the 2005 job re-roofed and deepened the rear addition around retained structure. (3) The superseded section records: "These foundations are not suitable for a two storey structure" — any first floor over the existing block footprint needs new foundations or an independent frame; design the two-storey element (and the 8 m rebuild, if chosen) with foundations engineered for future load from day one.

3.2 First floor rear — 4.00 m banked, 5–6 m push

The officer wrote: "The proposed extension would need to be reduced by 2m to 4m in depth" with "any roof design … match[ing] the existing hipped roof design". That is a written invitation for a 4.00 m × full-width two-storey rear extension — submit it drawn exactly to her words and refusal would require the council to contradict its own advice (the North Wiltshire consistency principle then bites at appeal). Your own architect's 45° analysis confirms 4 m clears the code from both neighbours on every construction he tested.

The push case for 5–6 m: no policy number stands against it (§2); the 45° line from No.117 runs from the conservatory end that already projects 0.73 m beyond your existing extension; No.113's obscure-glazed flank window is excluded from the code (not a main light source); the computed BRE numbers pass even at 6 m (VSC ratios 0.90–0.93 — screening fails but the numerical test that trumps it passes). Design levers that inspectors reward: hipped roof, ridge set well below the main ridge, first floor set in ~1 m from the No.117 boundary, chamfer or set-down over the last ~1.5 m at the No.113 corner (your architect's own stepped outline of 01.06.26 shows exactly this move). With the 8 m prior approval in hand, Mansell v Tonbridge & Malling [2017] EWCA Civ 1314 makes it a fallback the decision-maker must weigh: refusing the two-storey scheme achieves a worse outcome for the neighbours than what you can already build. Present the comparison in those terms.

Honest odds: 4 m ≈ formality. 5 m with full evidence pack ≈ good prospects at officer level, strong at appeal. 6 m ≈ likely refusal then a genuine appeal fight (householder appeals ran at ~36% allowed in 2025/26 nationally — but that is a base rate across all comers; a case armed with the LPA's own written concessions is far better placed). Recommended submission: 6.0 m ground / 4.5–5.0 m first floor, with the drawings pre-engineered to fall back to 4.0 m by condition rather than refusal.

3.3 Roof — 50 m³ under Class B

Class B permits +50 m³ of roof enlargement (detached; measured externally against the original roof) with no application, no neighbour consultation and no design discretion — conditions only: nothing above the existing ridge, nothing through the front roof plane, materials similar, side windows obscure-glazed, 0.2 m set-back up from the eaves. A full-width flat-roof rear box dormer is lawful; the set-back and original-wall limits are expressly disapplied for hip-to-gable enlargements and for an enlargement "which joins the original roof to the roof of a rear or side extension" — the L-shaped dormer over your new extension roofs. (Verified: that wording is original 2015 text, inherited from SI 2014/564 — it is settled law, not a recent novelty.) On a hipped roof, spend the 50 m³ on the rear dormer rather than hip-to-gable conversions, which eat the allowance and pick a needless design fight with the officer's hipped-roof position. Mansard: planning-only, officer hostile, skip. Class AA (extra full storeys): unavailable if the house predates 1 July 1948 — confirm the build date, but an inter-war house is almost certainly excluded (AA.1(c), confirmed unchanged by SI 2026/313).

3.3a Roof space — the maximisation plan

Design it as ONE roof: a unified crown roof (hipped perimeter at the existing pitch, flat centre just below the 7.52 m ridge) over house + extension, consented in the same householder application — no volume cap applies. The moves below are the PD fallbacks if the crown is resisted: (1) The new two-storey extension's roof is new build under planning permission — the 50 m³ Class B cap does not apply to it. Design it from day one with attic trusses, a usable loft and rear rooflights inside the hipped form the officer demands: at ~11 m × 4–5 m on plan that is a substantial extra floor-in-the-roof, delivered invisibly inside the "matching hipped roof" she asked for. (2) Spend the whole Class B 50 m³ on one full-width flat-roof box dormer in the main rear slope (face set 0.2 m up from the eaves, top below the 7.5 m ridge, cheeks within the flank walls): on this roof (eaves ~5.15–5.5 m, ridge ~7.5 m, ~11 m wide) a full-width dormer runs ≈40–48 m³ — inside the allowance, but the volume calc must be done off the measured survey before building. Do not hip-to-gable: it burns ~15–20 m³ of the same allowance for less floor area and picks a needless fight with the hipped-roof position. (3) Link the main-roof dormer to the extension's attic with an enlargement joining the two roofs — expressly permitted within the same 50 m³ (the "L-shaped dormer" carve-out in B.2(b)). (4) Class C rooflights (≤0.15 m proud) on the front and side slopes for light — uncapped. (5) The garage wing's flat roof is not part of the original main roof, so it neither consumes nor gains Class B volume. Net result: a dormered main loft plus a full attic storey over the new extension, all below the existing ridge, with the only discretionary piece being the extension roof — which travels inside the planning application you are already making.

3.4 Everything else

Side: the ~1.5 m gap to the No.113 boundary can be infilled single-storey (≤4 m high, eaves ≤3 m within 2 m of boundary, total side width ≤ half the original house). Front: nothing beyond the principal elevation except a 3 m² porch. Garden: Class E outbuildings up to 4 m (dual-pitched) — at 607 m² of plot the 50% cap leaves room for a very large garden room; keep it 2 m off boundaries or drop to 2.5 m height. Never: balconies/roof terraces (excluded from PD and a near-automatic privacy refusal at 0.78 m / 1.25 m gaps).
